FOUNDER / INDEPENDENT PUBLISHING / 2022–2025

Thy Academia

The project that taught me how much goes into making something of your own.

I founded Thy Academia in 2022, when I was already interested in research, education, writing, and the everyday difficulties that surround student life. I wanted a place where those interests could become accessible content rather than remain scattered ideas. Over the following three years, I developed it into an independent publishing project built around educational subjects, studying, and student wellbeing.

Because it was my own project, I couldn’t treat writing, design, planning, and publishing as separate departments. If a topic needed research, I did that work. If an article needed a clearer structure, I had to edit it. If a subject needed a visual explanation or a social post, I needed to decide what that should look like. Running the platform meant paying attention to the content itself and the practical questions that determined whether it could be published consistently.

Finding subjects worth writing about

Educational writing has a particular challenge: it can become either too vague to teach anything or so dense that people stop reading. I worked on finding a better middle ground. The objective was to make subjects about studying, personal development, and mental wellbeing understandable without treating the reader as someone who needed to be talked down to.

That involved researching topics, choosing angles, deciding how to introduce a subject, and organizing the material into a piece with a clear reason for existing. I became attentive to the difference between an interesting subject and an article that actually communicates something. A topic alone isn’t enough; you need to decide what the reader should understand by the time they finish.

One idea, more than one format

The platform also involved adapting content for different contexts. An article can make room for explanations and careful transitions; an Instagram post needs a more immediate entry point and a visual structure that can carry part of the meaning. Working across Medium and social content required me to think about those differences rather than simply cut an article into shorter sentences.

I developed graphics, social posts, and accompanying content while managing the overall presentation of Thy Academia. The work taught me to think about consistency in a practical way: not repeating the same layout or phrase, but making different pieces feel like they belong to the same project.

Learning to manage the whole thing

Founding a project is exciting when you’re coming up with ideas. Maintaining one requires a different kind of attention. I had to work through content planning, research, drafting, revisions, visual preparation, and publication without the structure of a larger team. Some of the most useful lessons were ordinary ones: a good idea still needs a realistic plan, a draft usually improves when you return to it, and taking responsibility for every stage gives you a much clearer understanding of how the stages affect each other.

Thy Academia ended in 2025, but it remains one of the clearest examples of my independent work. It was where I began learning how to turn personal curiosity into something organized, public, and sustained over time. A lot of what I do now in marketing and creative production still draws on that experience.
