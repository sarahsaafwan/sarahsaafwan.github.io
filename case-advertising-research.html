MARKETING RESEARCH / COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS / 2026

DAXUEN: Reading the competition

Looking at advertising with a question in mind.

Part of my marketing work with DAXUEN involved studying how other streetwear brands present themselves through paid advertising. That meant going beyond a general impression of a brand’s aesthetic and looking at the actual creative material it was putting in front of audiences: the kinds of products featured, the formats used, the message emphasized, and the role of styling, imagery, and promotional language.

The research focused on five competitors: Jaded London, Aelfric Eden, The Ragged Priest, mnml, and NAMED collective. I examined their publicly available advertising using Google Ads Transparency Center and Meta Ads Library, approaching each platform as a different window into the brands’ communication choices.

What I wanted to understand

An ad can be visually striking without telling you very much about the strategy behind it. I was interested in comparing patterns. Which brands leaned on product-focused creative, and which relied more on an overall fashion identity? How prominently did they use promotional messaging? How did the clothing itself appear on camera? Were they showing products plainly, building a lifestyle around them, or using particular creative formats to make the brand recognizable?

Rather than reducing each competitor to a generic strength or weakness, I organized the research brand by brand. This gave the work enough structure to compare approaches without assuming every company should advertise in exactly the same way.

Turning observations into useful research

A useful competitor review needs to separate what is observable from what is inferred. Seeing a particular creative approach in an advertising library does not tell you the company’s performance or revenue. It does, however, provide evidence of what that brand is choosing to communicate publicly. I used those visible examples as the basis for describing each advertiser’s approach and discussing possible lessons relevant to DAXUEN.

The work required organizing references, making comparisons between formats and creative styles, and summarizing findings so they could be discussed without losing the important details. I also considered what might be applicable to DAXUEN’s own content direction rather than treating competitors’ work as a template to copy.

What this work shows

Creative research isn’t separate from creative development. When done carefully, it helps you understand a category before making decisions within it. This project strengthened my ability to review real advertising examples, recognize repeated creative choices, distinguish evidence from opinion, and communicate observations in a way that can inform a marketing conversation.
