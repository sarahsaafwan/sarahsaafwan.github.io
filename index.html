SARAH SAFWAN / DIGITAL MARKETING & CREATIVE WORK

Hi, I’m Sarah.

I started making things long before I knew what to call any of it professionally. As a teenager, I was already interested in writing, researching subjects that caught my attention, and making little projects feel like something bigger than an idea sitting in my notes. I didn’t have a detailed career plan. I just kept finding things I wanted to create and learning whatever I needed to make them happen.

That curiosity became Thy Academia, an independent platform I founded and ran for three years. I wrote and researched articles, developed visual content, worked out how to present complicated subjects in accessible ways, and handled the practical reality of keeping an independent project moving. Later, I created a podcast, continued writing, explored short-form content, and started working more directly with brands. Each project required a different set of decisions, and together they helped me discover what kind of work I genuinely enjoy.

These days, my focus is digital marketing and creative work: the research behind an idea, the angle that makes it interesting, the way it should look and sound, and the decisions that turn it into something usable. I enjoy the early stages when you’re still figuring out what the work should be, but I also care about the less glamorous parts: reviewing details, correcting inconsistencies, improving an edit, and making sure the finished result actually serves its purpose.

This portfolio brings together professional work, independent projects, and ideas I’ve developed because I was curious enough to follow them through. They’re not all the same kind of work. That’s part of the point.

How I got here

I learned by starting things.

My work did not begin with a job title. It began with taking responsibility for projects that required me to figure things out as I went. Founding Thy Academia meant writing the content, deciding how to organize it, designing accompanying graphics, and keeping the whole platform coherent. Producing The Sae Talk brought in another kind of discipline: turning a topic into something that could be spoken about and listened to. Independent writing made me much more attentive to voice, structure, and whether an argument actually says something.

Working with brands added another layer. A personal idea can keep changing until it feels right to you; a brand project also has an audience, a brief, a visual identity, deliverables, and other people’s expectations. I started paying closer attention to the questions underneath a piece of content: Who is it for? What should somebody understand, feel, or do after seeing it? Is the visual idea actually achievable? Does it fit the brand without becoming interchangeable with everything else online?
