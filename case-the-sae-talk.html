INDEPENDENT PODCAST / COMPLETED PROJECT

The Sae Talk

Some subjects make more sense when you can hear someone think them through.

The Sae Talk was an independent podcast I created and hosted, beginning in 2024. It grew from my interest in conversations about personal experiences, perspective, and the things people often struggle to put into words. Writing had already given me a way to explore those subjects, but audio changed how I had to approach them. A written paragraph can be reread. In a podcast, the listener follows the thought as it unfolds, and the way you speak becomes part of the meaning.

I developed the podcast from its concept through to published episodes. My responsibilities included deciding on subjects, planning episode structure, preparing scripts or talking points, recording, editing, and thinking about how the show should be presented. It was both a creative project and an exercise in managing a production process that was different from the written and visual work I had done before.

Finding the right structure for a conversation

I wanted episodes to feel conversational rather than like someone reading an essay aloud. That required deciding what belonged in the introduction, which examples made a topic relatable, where a shift in the discussion felt natural, and when a point had been explored enough. There is a balance between sounding spontaneous and knowing where you’re going. Too much scripting makes the episode stiff; too little structure can leave the listener wondering what the point was.

The editing process brought another layer of decision-making. Spoken material has hesitations, repetitions, changes of pace, and moments that work differently when you listen back. Reviewing recordings taught me to pay attention not only to what I meant to say, but to what the listener would actually hear.

What the project added to my work

Producing The Sae Talk expanded my understanding of storytelling beyond the page. It required an awareness of tone, pacing, audience attention, and the relationship between preparation and a natural voice. Those are skills I continue to use when working with scripts, short-form video, and other content intended to sound human rather than overly rehearsed.

The Sae Talk is now a completed project. It remains part of my portfolio because it documents a meaningful period of independent audio production and the work involved in bringing a show from idea to release.
